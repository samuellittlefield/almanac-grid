How long BLM takes to decide solar right-of-way applications

Of solar applications filed with the Bureau of Land Management in {{ lead.early.first }}–{{ lead.early.last }}, {{ lead.early.pct|pct }} were decided within 12 months. Of those filed in {{ lead.late.first }}–{{ lead.late.last }}, {{ lead.late.pct|pct }} were.

Almanac Grid is a free tracker of federal permitting outcomes, built from public agency records. This page covers BLM rights-of-way for solar, wind, transmission and pipelines, and geothermal leases.

Read this first

  • The clock starts when an application arrived, not when it was complete. BLM records the date it received an application. It does not publish when an application was complete enough to process. Every elapsed time and overdue share on this page therefore overstates delay, by an unknown amount that may differ by project type and year.
  • Solar and wind denial and withdrawal rates are lower bounds. BLM closed {{ solar.outcomes.outcome_unclear_pct|pct }} of the solar decisions here, and {{ f.groups[1].outcomes.outcome_unclear_pct|pct }} of the wind decisions, without recording a grant, rejection or withdrawal. Some of those were likely rejections or withdrawals.
  • "Decided" means any final disposition: granted, rejected, withdrawn, or closed. It is not the same as approved.
  • The numbers are small. {{ solar.cohorts|map(attribute='filed')|min }} to {{ solar.cohorts|map(attribute='filed')|max }} solar applications a year, and {{ f.groups[1].cohorts|map(attribute='filed')|min }} to {{ f.groups[1].cohorts|map(attribute='filed')|max }} for wind. A few cases move a year's percentage by several points.
  • §1402 is part of the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), which passed the Senate on November 19, 2026, as an amendment to H.R. 2347. It is not law. The deadline comparison below applies the bill's test to past years. Under the bill only patterns after enactment would count, so these figures are not a finding under it.

Solar applications past the §1402 deadline

§1402 is part of BAAJA, which passed the Senate on November 19, 2026, as an amendment to H.R. 2347. It is not law. It would compare, for each project type, the share of applications pending more than 60 days past their deadline with the same share over a lookback period of earlier years. Here the deadline is 1 year from filing, or 2 years for cases with an environmental impact statement (EIS). The chart shows that share for the open queue at the end of each year, next to two baselines: the previous 5 year ends pooled, which is the introduced text's lookback, and the previous 10 year ends pooled, which is the lookback reportedly in the Senate-passed version (not yet verified against the passed text). About this project

I built Almanac Grid to see what a federal permitting bill would actually measure. §1402 of BAAJA would let a developer point to a statistical pattern of denial or delay against its project type. No one publishes those numbers by project type in one place: the records are spread across separate agency systems. So I built the dataset.

  • What it is: a database of federal permitting decisions from FERC and BLM, normalized into one model: a project, and every agency action on it. That shape is what makes a project's total time across agencies measurable, the number that would show whether the bill's NEPA reforms worked.
  • Who it's for: energy project developers, their regulatory counsel, and the permitting consultants and law firms who advise them.
  • What I left out, and why: Army Corps wetlands permits are mostly available only through FOIA, so they wait until there's demand for that turnaround. Geospatial siting tools are a crowded market, so they're out of scope.
  • How it's built: Python, PostgreSQL and FastAPI, reading FERC's eLibrary and three BLM systems, one of which has no public API. This page is built from a frozen, checksummed snapshot so every number can be traced. I worked with Claude, Anthropic's AI model, to analyze the bill and write the code.

Methodology

Sources

  • BLM national case GIS layers for rights-of-way and geothermal leases: which cases exist, their product type, states and current disposition.
  • BLM's Mineral & Land Records System (MLRS) case actions: the application date and the outcome, from BLM's own disposition action codes.
  • BLM ePlanning (the National NEPA Register): the environmental review tier (categorical exclusion, EA or EIS), matched on case file number.

All three are public. The data was last fetched on {{ as_of.strftime('%B %-d, %Y') }}.

Definitions

  • Filing date: the earliest "Application Received" action in MLRS, or "Case Established" if there is none. It is never estimated.
  • Decision: the first action that grants, rejects or withdraws the application. A case closed without one is "closed, no reason recorded." Later amendments, renewals or relinquishments don't change it.
  • Decided within 12 months: a final disposition of any kind within 365 days of the filing date, as a share of all applications filed that year with a filing date, including those still pending.
  • Overdue: open more than 60 days past the deadline. Deadline = filing date plus 2 years for cases with an EIS, plus 1 year otherwise. §1402 counts from a complete application, which these records don't show.
  • Baseline, 5 years for a year: the five previous year-end open queues pooled (total overdue over total open). This is the lookback in §1402's introduced text. First shown for 2021, the first year with five previous year ends in the data.
  • Baseline, 10 years for a year: the ten previous year-end open queues pooled the same way, using whichever of them exist from {{ 2016 }} on. This is the lookback reportedly in the Senate-passed version of §1402; it has not yet been verified against the passed text. The data starts in {{ 2016 }}, so {{ as_of.year }} is the only year with a full 10-year window: 2021 pools 5 year ends (the same as its 5-year baseline), 2022 pools 6, and so on to 2025, which pools 9. Shown for the same years as the 5-year baseline.
  • Status at 36 months: an application's outcome if BLM decided it within 36 months of its filing date, and "still pending" otherwise. Only applications at least 36 months old on {{ as_of.strftime('%B %-d, %Y') }} count, so every filing period is compared at the same age. One grant is dated before its filing date ({{ s36.decided_before_filing|join(', ') }}); it counts as granted within 36 months.
  • Review tier is known only where an ePlanning project lists the case. Cases without one are treated as non-EIS.

What's excluded

  • Cases decided before {{ 2016 }} are not loaded. Applications filed before {{ 2016 }} appear only in the open-queue counts, if still open.
  • Records whose decision date is in the future or before the filing date are dropped ({% for g in f.groups[:5] %}{{ g.label }} {{ g.dropped_impossible_dates }}{{ '; ' if not loop.last else '' }}{% endfor %}), except in the 36-month table (see "Status at 36 months").
  • Cases with no filing date count in the outcome table but not in timing ({% for g in f.groups[:5] %}{{ g.label }} {{ g.without_filing_date }}{{ '; ' if not loop.last else '' }}{% endfor %}).
  • Some large projects are missing from BLM's GIS layers (presumably because their lands couldn't be mapped) and so aren't in the data.
  • Wind and solar site-testing permits, temporary use permits and FERC decisions are not on this page.
