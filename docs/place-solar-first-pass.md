# Place layer, first pass: Southwest solar on federal land

November 24, 2026. Analysis only; the public page is unchanged.

**Question.** What share of new solar PV capacity in CA, NV, AZ, NM and UT
sits on federal land, by year?

**Short answer.** From 2016 through October 2026, **17%** of the 38.3 GW of
new PV capacity in the five states sits on federal land: 14% on BLM land and
3% on other federal land: the Edwards Sanborn plants on Edwards AFB, plus
Centennial Flats, which is probably misassigned. The share swings a lot from year
to year because a few large BLM projects dominate it. It was under 2% in 2018,
2019 and 2025 and 23–28% in 2020 and 2022–2024. Nevada is the most federal
state (36%), then California (21%) and Arizona (12%). New Mexico (1%) and Utah
(0%) build almost entirely on private, state and tribal land. Planned projects
lean more federal: **24%** of 20.2 GW (22% BLM), and 81% in Nevada. Two
questionable EIA points account for about 1 percentage point of each total
(see "Looks off"). Without them, the figures are 16% operating and 22–24%
planned.

"Federal" here means BLM plus other federal agencies. Tribal trust land (BIA in
the SMA layer) is counted separately and is **not** included in the federal
share.

Most of the operating plants without a footprint came online in 2025–2026,
after USPVDB's cutoff. For the 730 plants that have both a footprint and a

### Tracker cross-check: BLM solar ROW grants, 2016–2023

The tracker has 49 solar right-of-way grants in the five states decided
2016–2023. Each was matched to an EIA plant within 1 km of the case's BLM
polygon, preferring a plant whose name shares a distinctive word with the case
name or customer.

- **38 of 49 (78%) match an operating or planned plant.** 37 of those plants
  (76% of all grants) are assigned to BLM land. Excluding the two grants that
  aren't for a PV plant (Heliogen, which is concentrating solar, and the Desert
  Quartzite gen-tie), the rate is 37 of 47 (79%).
- The 38 matched grants cover just **22 plants**. Projects routinely hold
  several serials (Dry Lake has 5 NV Energy cases, Harry Allen 3, Yellow Pine
  4 including the tortoise fence), so a grant count overstates projects.
- The one match not on BLM land: **Townsite Solar 2** (NVNV105860618, 20
  acres). It is next to Townsite Solar (Boulder City land, PVT in the SMA), so
  it is probably the project's line or access across BLM land.

## Looks off

1. **The SMA layer has no private category in California.** It codes nearly
   all non-federal, non-state land there as "undetermined" (UND, 196,000
   km²; PVT covers only 2,300 km²). That is why 15.3 GW of California capacity
   sits on "undetermined" land. The federal share is unaffected. The tables
   combine private and undetermined, and California's "private" really means
   "not federal, state or tribal".
2. **Centennial Flats (AZ, 500 MW, 2026) is probably not on Reclamation
   land.** Its EIA point falls on a 1.1 km² Reclamation polygon with a
   10.8 km perimeter, a narrow strip that is almost certainly the Central
   Arizona Project canal. It is 14 m from the polygon's edge. A 500 MW plant
   doesn't fit there. It accounts for most of 2026's federal share.
3. **Southern Bighorn Solar Hybrid (NV, 300 MW planned 2028) lands inside
   Lake Mead NRA.** The point is more than 2 km from any non-NPS land. Its
   sister project, Southern Bighorn Solar 2, lands on the Moapa River
   reservation, which is probably where this one belongs too.
4. **Two planned plants have EIA points in the ocean:** Cobalt (Riverside
   County, 250 MW) and Athens Solar I (listed in Placer County, but the point
   is off Big Sur, 250 MW). Both are "unmapped". Cobalt holds a BLM grant, so
   it is probably federal.
5. **Effect of 2–4.** Dropping Centennial Flats and Southern Bighorn and
   counting Cobalt as BLM moves the operating total from 17.3% to 16.0% and
   the planned total from 23.8% to 23.6%. None of these are corrected in the
   tables; the stored assignments are the method's output.
6. **Some tracker grant dates come long after the plant was built.** The four
   Blythe Solar grants are dated June 7, 2023, but the plants they match came
   online in 2016–2020. Likewise, Soda Spring Valley (2022) matches Luning
   Energy (2017, same applicant, so probably an expansion EIA doesn't list
   yet). The Blythe records look like reissued or replacement grants, not the
   approvals that allowed construction. The §1402 analysis counts them as 2023
   approvals, which is worth checking.
7. **Footprints are panel-array outlines, not right-of-way boundaries.** A
   plant's array can sit entirely on private land while its BLM grant covers
   access, lines or other parts of the site. The match rate above treats that
   as a match.

## Method

- **Universe.** EIA-860M generators with Technology "Solar Photovoltaic" in the
  five states. That means the Operating sheet with operating year 2016 or
  later, plus the Planned sheet. No PV generator in these states that came
  online since 2016 has retired. Generators are aggregated to the plant (Plant
  ID). A plant is kept if its total PV nameplate is at least 1 MW, which
  dropped 15 plants totalling 11.7 MW. Capacity counts in each generator's own
  year, so a phased plant adds MW in several years but has one owner.
- **Footprints.** USPVDB v4.0 polygons, joined on `eia_id` = Plant ID: 730 of
  1,005 plants. The rest use EIA's coordinates.
- **Ownership.** The SMA `SurfaceManagementAgency` layer, made valid and split
  into single polygons; areas and distances are computed in EPSG:5070.
  - Categories: `blm`; `other_federal` (every other federal agency except
    BIA); `tribal` (BIA trust land); `state`; `private`; plus the minor
    `local`, `undetermined` and `unmapped` (no SMA polygon).
  - A footprint plant takes the category with the most area, and stores every
    category's share.
  - A point plant takes the category of the polygon containing the point.
  - "Within 500 m of a boundary" means land of any other category, or
    unmapped land, lies within 500 m. A line between two polygons of the same
    category doesn't count.
- **Cross-check.** Case polygons come from BLM's public ROW case layers (all
  four dispositions), because the tracker stores no geometry.

## Where it lives

- Code: `analysis/place_fetch.py` downloads the inputs. `python -m
  analysis.place_solar build` loads and assigns them, and `report` prints the
  tables above. Both need the extra packages in `requirements-place.txt`.
- New tables, which are dropped and rebuilt on each build. No existing table
  is modified.
  - `place_source`: one row per source, with URL, version, SHA-256 and
    download time.
  - `place_solar_plant`: one row per plant, with owner, federal and BLM
    shares, point-based owner, boundary distance and geometry as WKT.
  - `place_solar_capacity`: MW by plant, status (operating or planned) and
    year.
  - `place_solar_land_share`: footprint area by plant and owner category or
    agency.
  - `place_blm_case_check`: the grant-by-grant cross-check.
- Raw files are in `data/place/2026-11-24/` (not in git), along with
  `sources.json`.
